# v0.3: the connections around a gift, and the people around a donor

**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Packages changed:**

- Open Impact Core, no package version created (namespace deferred, plan Section 4.3)
- Open Impact Giving, no package version created

This is the internal iteration report. The admin-facing lines are collected in
`v0.3-draft-addresses.md`, `v0.3-draft-relationships-affiliations.md` and
`v0.3-draft-commitments.md`, and are assembled into the public release notes when a version
is promoted.

Read the two honesty sections first: **Org shapes passing** and **Open risks**. Every Apex
test written in this iteration is unexecuted, one soft credit rule collision was found and
decided during it, and one consequence of that collision is still open.

**This iteration ran alongside v0.1 and v0.2 rather than after them**, at the owner's
instruction and under plan Section 9.6. Plan Section 6 says not to start an iteration until
the previous iteration's package version is promoted; no package version exists, so the
sequencing rule was relaxed and the per feature discipline kept instead: canonical model
first, one branch per feature, review against plan Sections 7.4 and 9.4, squash merge. C-17
in particular was built during the v0.1 window and is reported as v0.3 work here because that
is the iteration plan Section 5 assigns it to. Three release note lines that plan Section 7.4
requires were never written as drafts, for G-08, G-09 and G-10; this report is written from
the merge records, the canonical model and the admin guide pages instead.

## Features completed against the plan

| ID | Feature | Package | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-15 | Relationships, contact to contact, reciprocal, typed | Core | Eleven shipped relationship type mappings, none gendered: Parent becomes Child, Grandparent becomes Grandchild, and Spouse, Partner, Sibling, Friend and Colleague become themselves. The reciprocal side is written inside an `AutomationControl` bypass cleared in a `finally`, not behind a depth counter, so the mirror is built complete in one save. On insert the service pairs with an unpaired record that already is the other side, which is what makes an import carrying both sides settle into one pair rather than four records. When both sides are edited in one save the lower id wins, so the result is the same every time. Mirroring can be switched off for the whole org or for one record. |
| C-16 | Affiliations, contact to organization, role, primary, dates | Core | One primary affiliation per person, maintained in the same save, with the organization named on the person's Primary Affiliation field. An ended affiliation loses the primary badge and empties that field. A second affiliation for the same person, organization and role whose dates overlap one on file is refused by name. |
| C-17 | Addresses with default propagation to standard fields | Core | Many addresses per household, organization or person, one marked default and copied into the standard address fields so mail merges and other apps keep reading what they always read. An existing address on a household or organization becomes its first address record. A setting decides whether editing one person's address moves the whole household or gives that person an address of their own, defaulting to the household. Seasonal addresses with date ranges, including ranges crossing New Year, can be entered now; the automatic swap is C-18 in v0.4. `Verification_Status__c` and `Latest_Verified_Date__c` are never written by packaged code, so a verification app can own them (R-AD6). |
| G-07 | Commitments with generated installments, balances, overdue and upcoming views | Giving | Pledges and recurring gifts with real installment records rather than a calculation, so a schedule can be reported on. Overdue, Upcoming 30 days, Active pledges and Active recurring ship as list views. A gift naming a commitment but not an installment is applied to the oldest unpaid one, which is a setting. Pause leaves scheduled installments alone, cancel marks unpaid ones Skipped, and nothing received is ever changed or deleted. A year of installments is kept in front of an open ended schedule by a nightly job that also marks payments overdue after a grace period. Four settings in the console: horizon, grace days, automatic application, and a read only stamp of the last nightly run. |
| G-08 | Soft credits with automatic household member credits | Giving | Recognition is kept separate from money, so receipts and bank reconciliation are untouched. A gift from one household member credits every other current member, controlled by one setting. R-SC3 works in both membership modes, through a handler on the membership object and on Contact, so a membership change recredits and turning the setting off removes the automatic credits it created. What a refund does to a soft credit changed during this iteration: see ADR-0023 under Open risks. |
| G-09 | Tributes: honor, memorial, notification recipient | Giving | The honoree, the person to notify, the donor's message and whether the notification has gone out, with a panel on the gift record page. A tribute never states the amount, because the letter to a bereaved family says a gift was made in memory of their mother, not what it was worth. The deceased check reads both Contact and Account, so it applies in a person account org. |
| G-10 | Matching gift linkage, employer gift to employee gift | Giving | The link is set from the company's gift and filled in on the other side, visible from both. The employee is soft credited on the company's gift, so their influence shows in donor reporting without crediting them with money they did not give. A gift that has been refunded or written off cannot be half of a match (R-G11). Open Impact does not tell you which employers match: that is a database, and it is out of scope. |
| G-11 | Pledge balance and commitment rollups | Giving | Five rollup definition records: the pledge balance on household, organization and person, plus Commitment Paid To Date and Installment Paid Amount. They were held back from the commitments merge because `Rollup_Definition_Default__mdt` did not yet exist, and deployed with the C-13 adapter in v0.2. The three pledge balance rows filter on commitment status, which is why ADR-0022's status change correctly left them alone. |

## Open risks

**A refunded employer gift still leaves its matched donor credit standing.** Settled after
this iteration closed: ADR-0031 decided that the refund does propagate along the link, and
the paragraph below is left as the record of what was open at the end of v0.3. This was the one
open defect this iteration knows about and did not fix. R-G11 creates a Matched Donor credit
when two gifts are linked as a match, and removes it only when the link is undone. The
negative gift that records a refund is not itself half of a matching pair, so it produces no
negative matched donor credit. Refund the employer's gift and the employee keeps the full
matched recognition. It is recorded in ADR-0023's consequences and belongs with the matching
gift feature, G-10 in plan Section 5; ADR-0023's text names G-09, which is Tributes, and that
reference is wrong. Fixing it means deciding whether a refund propagates along the match link,
which is a decision rather than a repair. Owned by the builder, for the owner's call.

**The soft credit rule collision, found and decided during this iteration.** Two rules both
fired on a full refund and undid each other. R-SC5 gives the negative refund gift its own
negative household credits; R-SC6 recomputed the original gift's automatic credits when its
status changed and treated a Refunded gift as not recognizable, so it deleted the positive
credit. Both ran. A household member recognized for a gift of 250 that was refunded in full
read **-250, not 0**: the reversal was applied twice.

ADR-0023 keeps the credits. A gift now keeps the automatic soft credits it earned however its
status later changes, and the only reversal is the negative credit on the negative gift.
`SoftCreditService` no longer reads gift status at all. A fully refunded gift therefore shows
a visible pair, +250 and -250, and the total reads **0**; a partial refund of 100 against 250
reads **150**, which is what it always did and what the rejected alternative would have broken.
Recognition now reverses exactly the way money does under R-G3, and ADR-0022's widened rollup
filter is what makes the pair net correctly, so no rollup change was needed. A household
member recognized on one fully refunded gift reads one credit and a total of zero, the same
shape as Gift Count and Total Giving.

The risk that remains is the same one v0.2 recorded: this was a defect in what a number says
rather than in whether something works, it was found by a neighboring feature rather than by a
gate, and no static check will catch the next one. Owned by the builder.

**Nothing has been deployed.** The Core stage of the staged deploy fails with an
`UNKNOWN_EXCEPTION`, zero components deployed and zero component errors. Until it passes,
every behavior described above is reasoned from the code rather than observed, and ADR-0023
says so in its own consequences. Owned by the builder, in progress in a separate session.

**Untested code paths that change other people's records.** The relationship service writes
the other side of every relationship under an automation bypass, the affiliation service moves
the primary badge between records, the address service propagates a default onto standard
fields and can move a whole household, and the nightly installment job writes in batches. None
has executed. The address propagation in particular writes to the standard Account and Contact
address fields, which every other app in the org reads. Do not point any of this at real data
before the Apex tests have run in an org. Owned by Brandon, as the person who will be tempted.

**Both membership modes are claimed and neither is proven.** Relationships, affiliations, soft
credits and tributes all read and write a person through a Contact reference or an Account
reference with exactly one set, per plan Section 6.3, and the duplicate and self checks compare
a prefixed key so a contact and an account can never collide. That design is the reason the
first customers can be Agentforce Nonprofit orgs, and it has never run against an org where
people actually are accounts. The Person Accounts shape is the one to point the test org at
first. Owned by the builder.

**The vendored library's attribution will not survive packaging.** Unchanged from `v0.2.md`:
a plain `LICENSE` file is not a Salesforce metadata type, so apex-rollup's MIT text will not
travel into a package version, and this iteration's pledge balance and recognition totals run
through that engine like every other total.
`packages/core/vendor/apex-rollup/VENDOR.md` names three ways to carry the notice. Owned by
the builder, at first packaging.

**The `nonprofit-cloud` shape's feature string is unverified.** `NonprofitCloud` in
`config/scratch-defs/nonprofit-cloud.json` is a reasonable guess, not a tested value. An
entitled Dev Hub will either create the org or list the feature names it can grant. Owned by
Brandon, who holds the entitlement question.

**The name, the licence and branch protection** are unchanged from `v0.1.md` and still owned
by Brandon. They are not repeated here.

## Install and walk through this iteration

No package version exists, because the namespace is deferred and no 2GP version has been
created. There is no install link. This is a source deploy, and step 2 is expected to fail
today.

1. Create an org from the Person Accounts shape, which is the one this iteration most needs:
   `scripts/org/create-scratch-org.sh person-accounts dev --days 30 --replace`.
2. Deploy in stages: `scripts/org/deploy-packages.sh dev`. **Expect the Core stage to fail
   with an `UNKNOWN_EXCEPTION` until the session working on that has finished.**
3. Assign `Nonprofit_Admin`, then `Nonprofit_Admin_Group`, then `Giving_Admin`.
4. Open the Nonprofit Hub, walk the Setup Assistant, and set the coexistence mode to
   Agentforce Nonprofit, which moves household membership to the junction records person
   accounts need in the same save.
5. Load the sample data from Nonprofit Settings, General, Sample Data, in junction mode.
6. Walk the admin guide pages in this order:
   [addresses](../admin-guide/addresses.md),
   [relationships](../admin-guide/relationships.md),
   [affiliations](../admin-guide/affiliations.md),
   [commitments](../admin-guide/commitments.md),
   [soft credits](../admin-guide/soft-credits.md),
   [tributes](../admin-guide/tributes.md),
   [matching gifts](../admin-guide/matching-gifts.md).
   Each has a five minute walkthrough and a "common mistakes" section. Assign the shipped
   Gift Record Page as the org default for Gift first: the soft credit and tribute panels live
   on it.
7. What to check that the pages do not say, in this order of usefulness:
   - Record that Harper is Luis's spouse on Harper's record, then open Luis. The other side
     must already be there, exactly one record, with no duplicate. Give it an end date and
     both sides must read Former.
   - Enter a gift of 250 from one household member, check the other member's soft credit,
     then refund the whole 250. The member must end at **0** with two visible rows, +250 and
     -250, not at -250 and not at nothing. That is the defect this iteration found and
     decided, and it is the single most valuable thing to verify.
   - Link an employer's gift to an employee's gift, then refund the employer's gift. The
     matched donor credit will still be standing at full amount. That is the known open
     defect above, and seeing it is confirmation rather than a surprise.
   - Create a pledge of 1,200 over twelve months, pay one installment with a gift naming only
     the commitment, and check that the oldest unpaid installment is the one marked paid and
     that the pledge balance drops by exactly that amount.
   - Move one person in a household to a new address and check the setting did what it says:
     the whole household moves, or only that person does.
   - The Error Log should be empty. An entry after a clean walkthrough is the most useful
     thing this iteration could hand you.
